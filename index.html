CourtVision

Automated Basketball Highlight Editor

It watches a half-hour basketball highlight video and edits it down to a half-minute edit-styled clip, picking the plays, following the players, and cutting it on beat with the music.

Editing a highlight reel by hand takes hours, picking out the good plays trimming each one so the basket lands where you want it, re-cropping every shot for a phone screen, then lining the whole thing up with a song.

CourtVision does all of it on its own. It takes in a long highlight compilation, a player and a song, and it hands back a finished short edit-styled clip.

These six edits were made through this project, using six players, six songs, footage spanning three decades. Every play, cut, crop and bit of timing was chosen by the software. Sound on.

What it sees

One reel with the software's view drawn on top.

  • everyone on court
  • the player this clip is about
  • …when he slips out of frame
  • the phone-shaped crop it keeps
  • the ball
  • the rim

Four models do the looking, and all of them ran locally on my laptop. One finds the people and the ball. One finds the rim One watches for the moment the broadcast cuts to a different camera, so a clip never exists between two shots. One reads the number on a jersey, which is how a reel about one player stays on that player.

The green box is the part that survives into the finished reel. It tracks the player down the court, which is how a wide television shot becomes a vertical video without losing him.

The five stages

~40,000 lines of Python, running offline on one machine. Unless specified, the same video and the same song always produce the same edit, frame for frame.

More technical detail is in ARCHITECTURE.md and METHOD.md.

The reels are highlight footage cut to commercial music, shown as examples of what the software produces.
